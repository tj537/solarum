# SOUNDS – how making a sound works

One screen for everyone: a beginner gets a good sound in two clicks, a pro gets every knob.
Each step down the list gives you more control; none of them is in the way of the next.

## 1. Start from something that already works

**+ New sound** (⌘N) opens *Make a sound* – also what an empty library shows, so the first
visit never starts on a blank page.

- **Start from a track** – eight cards in the tracks' colours, each with a picture of its sound.
  - *Paint over it*: the track's template on the drawing board (`library::template`).
  - *Edit the real one*: the kit's own sound as it plays now, in the waveform editor (`engine::render_hit`).
- **Or start from** – blank board, surprise me, record, import.

## 2. Paint it

The drawing board shows sound as two pictures you can paint: **PITCH** (the line) and
**LOUDNESS** (the bars under it). A kick is a falling line with fading bars – you see why it
sounds like a kick, and changing it is drawing. You hear it after every stroke.

Safety nets so trying is free: undo/redo, Reset (back to the template), Clear, Back.
The track (FOR) sets the colour and where the sound goes.

## 3. Shape it

*Make it a sound* hands it to the editor: cut, fades, pitch, reverse, tone, crush – the same
picture tiles and sliders as the main window. Save puts it on its track.

## Principles

- Pictures over numbers: every control shows what it does (tiles, lanes, thumbnails).
- Always audible: every change plays at once; Space plays, Loop keeps it going.
- Nothing is lost: undo everywhere, drafts until Save.
- The track colours tie the SOUNDS screen to the main window.

## Next ideas

- Templates per vibe (a dub kick, a hard kick …) or several per track.
- *Paint over the real one*: a loudness lane on top of any recorded or imported sound.
- A pitch grid that snaps the line to the song's scale for melodic sounds.
